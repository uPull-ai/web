The Rulebook Gets a Timetable

Last week's issue showed delivery moving ahead of direction, with NHS England's board recording that no single clear vision for AI exists. This week the government supplies part of that direction, with dates attached. It has accepted all 44 recommendations of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare, with draft guidance due by December 2026 and a full roadmap by Spring 2027. The MHRA has opened the third phase of its AI Airlock sandbox, and Berkshire Healthcare's ambient voice tender reaches its first deadlines this week. Two start ups in a UKRI announcement show where AI agents and rehabilitation robotics begin. Each story carries a date you can put in a diary.

1 Agentic AI

A quiet week for new NHS agent deployments. We found none inside our sourcing standard, so this section carries one early stage company and one regulatory signal.

Headline AI development

UKRI reported on 8 October that seven health start ups have joined the Science and Technology Facilities Council's DeepTech Catalyst Health programme at Daresbury Laboratory. One of them, Asika, is developing AI agents for hospitals and clinics that automate administrative workflows, with the aim of reducing paperwork and improving operational efficiency. Each company receives a support package worth up to £70,000. The programme launched in 2024 with £1.8 million from the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority Innovation Zone Programme. The announcement describes work in development. It names no NHS site, no deployment and no results.

Source: UKRI, STFC backs health start ups to improve lives · HTN, Seven health tech start ups to receive up to £70,000
Governance or ethics note

The MHRA's AI Airlock Phase 3 call, published on 6 October, sets post market surveillance as its priority challenge area. One of its three topics asks how monitoring can detect and respond to safety or performance signals in settings with varying levels of clinical or organisational visibility. The page gives remote, direct to consumer and higher autonomy systems as examples, and refers to contexts with limited human oversight. The call is scoped to AI enabled medical devices. The link to administrative agents is uPull.ai's reading.

Source: GOV.UK, AI Airlock Phase 3 application
Try this in a workflow

Choose one automation or agent that your organisation uses or is considering. Write three lines: who sees its errors, how quickly they see them, and who can switch it off. Share the three lines with your clinical safety officer or information governance lead this week.

2 Robotics AI

No new surgical robotics announcement met our sourcing standard this week. This section reports one early stage rehabilitation device from the same UKRI announcement.

Headline AI development

The UKRI announcement of 8 October includes HMA Technologies, which is developing a wearable robotic arm support. The aim is to help stroke survivors carry out intensive rehabilitation exercises independently at home, supporting recovery beyond traditional clinical settings. The company joins the DTC Health incubation programme at Sci-Tech Daresbury in the Liverpool City Region. Eligible companies for the programme are based in the Liverpool City Region, are less than five years old and focus on health and life sciences.

Source: UKRI, STFC backs health start ups to improve lives
Governance or ethics note

The source says the device could help stroke survivors, which describes potential. It reports no trial data, no regulatory status and no NHS pathway. A device used independently at home raises questions for any future pathway: who reviews the exercise data, and who responds when the device or the patient's progress shows a problem. Those questions are uPull.ai's own, and the source does not address them.

Source: UKRI, STFC backs health start ups to improve lives
Try this in a workflow

If your trust runs a stroke or neurorehabilitation service, ask your lead therapist for a one page list of any home rehabilitation devices already in use. Record for each device who reviews the data it produces and how often.

3 Ambient AI

A trust publishes its ambient voice requirements and its timetable, and shows how it is building staff confidence in AI alongside.

Headline AI development

Berkshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust published a pre tender notice on 10 September for an ambient voice technology solution valued at £3.24 million. The contract runs for two years with optional extensions for two more, and the trust is buying through the Artificial Intelligence Dynamic Purchasing System RM6200. The notice requires every bidder to be certified on NHS England's Ambient Voice Technology Self-Certified Supplier Registry. The trust intends to create a supplier shortlist on 13 October and issue a capability assessment on 14 October, to be returned by 21 October. Tender documents are planned for 10 November, and the trust reserves the right to amend these dates. We read the notice through the Stotles tender aggregator, which reproduces Find a Tender record 085619-2026.

Source: Stotles, Ambient Voice Technology pre tender notice · Find a Tender, notice 085619-2026
Healthcare or NHS use case

HTN reported on 5 October that Berkshire Healthcare is delivering face to face Copilot training aboard its Health Bus, using practical demonstrations and showing staff how to create emails, presentations, summaries and reports. The final roadshow session runs at Wokingham Hospital on 26 October 2026, and trust colleagues book through the digital learning hub. HTN also records the trust's five year strategy, which cites an initial income and expenditure planning gap of £15 million for 2026/27 and a two percent annual productivity growth requirement. The trust expects AI ambient documentation, electronic record integration and process automation to help. Reading the tender and the training together, one buyer is preparing its supplier requirements and its workforce in the same autumn. That reading is uPull.ai's.

Source: HTN, Berkshire Healthcare launches face to face Copilot training
Governance or ethics note

The notice makes registry certification a condition of entry for every bidder. It also asks for a system that supports inpatient wards, outpatient clinics and community settings, with background noise, shared spaces and variable connectivity, and consultations involving carers, family members or interpreters with accurate speaker differentiation. The stated aims include higher quality documentation, reduced errors and shorter turnaround for clinic letters, while maintaining high standards of clinical safety and transparency. The notice sets out requirements and says nothing about how performance will be checked after go live.

Source: Stotles, Ambient Voice Technology pre tender notice
Try this in a workflow

Check your own ambient voice supplier against the NHS England registry and record the date you checked. Then ask the supplier to show how its output handles a consultation with an interpreter or carer present, and write down what you see.

4 Clinical AI

The government turns a regulatory blueprint into a programme with dates, and the MHRA opens a sandbox for monitoring AI devices after deployment.

Headline AI development

On 6 October the government confirmed that it accepts all 44 recommendations of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare, which the Commission published on 10 September. Delivery will run across the UK under a newly established Programme Board, with ten priority areas. The response sets three dated steps. The MHRA will issue draft guidance by December 2026 on managing changes to AI enabled medical devices as they adapt and improve. It will consult in 2027 on how AI enabled devices are qualified and classified, which was the Commission's first recommendation. A full implementation roadmap with timelines and responsibilities follows by Spring 2027. The government is also exploring staged authorisation pathways that would let promising tools enter NHS use earlier under close supervision. We covered the Commission's report in our 29 September to 5 October issue. This story is the government's reply to it.

Source: GOV.UK, Government backs recommendations of NHS doctors-led AI Commission · GOV.UK, Government response to the National Commission's recommendations · HTN, Government accepts National Commission 44 recommendations
Healthcare or NHS use case

The MHRA opened applications for the third phase of AI Airlock on 6 October. Phase 3 focuses on post market surveillance and lifecycle regulation, and the programme is funded until April 2029. Applications received before 31 October join the current review wave, the window stays open after that date, and a further shortlisting period follows in Spring 2027. The MHRA expects to select its first wave of innovators in November 2026. There is no application fee. Candidates fund their own studies and testing, including data access, and should expect a testing plan of 6 to 12 months. Testing can use a simulation roundtable, a virtual test space, or a real world setting run outside patient pathways, such as shadow deployment. Organisations of all sizes may apply, and a lead applicant may partner with other organisations.

Source: GOV.UK, AI Airlock Phase 3 application · GOV.UK, Government backs recommendations of NHS doctors-led AI Commission
Governance or ethics note

Professor Henrietta Hughes, Deputy Chair of the Commission and Patient Safety Commissioner for England, welcomed the commitments to monitor AI throughout its use in practice, to strengthen patient involvement in decision making and to provide clear routes to redress when care falls below expectations. The government release also refers to new commitments to keep patients informed about when AI is used in their care. The response describes work still to come, with draft guidance, a consultation and a roadmap all dated ahead.

Source: GOV.UK, Government backs recommendations of NHS doctors-led AI Commission
Try this in a workflow

For each AI enabled tool live in your organisation, record who receives post deployment performance information from the supplier and what result would make you act. Compare your list with the three Airlock topics: deployment context, varying levels of local oversight, and communication of updates and rollbacks.

5 Learning & Development

Three ways to build AI skills this month: an online conference day this week, an open clinical fellowship, and a webinar for organisations weighing an application to the MHRA sandbox.

AI in the NHS 2026, The Health Foundation

A full day online event on how the NHS can implement AI at scale responsibly, safely and effectively. It is the organiser's third annual AI event.

Who it's for
Stakeholders from the NHS, government, industry, academia and the charity sector, according to the organiser.
How to access
Register through the Health Foundation event page, which links to a Zoom registration. The page we reviewed does not state a fee.
What it covers
Translating national strategy into implementation, evaluating and scaling AI tools, building trust among clinicians, patients and the public, and supporting the workforce. Listed speakers include Brian Anderson of the Coalition for Health AI, Ricardo Baptista Leite of HealthAI and Ed Middleton, Interim Director of Strategy at the MHRA.
Deadline
Thursday 15 October 2026, 10:00 to 17:00.
Source: The Health Foundation, AI in the NHS 2026

NHS Fellowship in Clinical AI, Cohort 6

A 12 month programme at 0.4 FTE, which is two days a week, for clinicians and other professionals who want to learn the safe deployment and evaluation of AI in clinical workflows.

Who it's for
Registered health and social care professionals with an NHS employer apply by the competitive route. Health and social care staff providing NHS services can apply by the nominated route.
How to access
Both routes use one application form, and your Approver submits an Approval in Principle form by the same deadline. Nominated applicants arrange the £8,000 course fee through a sponsor or self funding.
What it covers
Fellows join existing NHS clinical AI projects under a supervisor, attend compulsory masterclass workshops and receive clinical safety training at Digital Clinical Safety Practitioner level. Cohort 5 had 310 eligible applicants for 34 competitively allocated posts.
Deadline
Applications close on 16 November 2026 at 23:45 GMT. Interviews run from 13 to 29 January 2027, and Cohort 6 starts on 11 August 2027.
Source: NHS Fellowship in Clinical AI, apply page

MHRA AI Airlock Phase 3 applicant webinar

A webinar for prospective applicants to the third phase of the MHRA's regulatory sandbox for AI enabled medical devices.

Who it's for
Developers of eligible AI enabled medical devices, and organisations that may partner with them. The application page invites organisations of all sizes.
How to access
Full details are on the MHRA application page linked below.
What it covers
The Phase 3 priority topics on post market surveillance and lifecycle oversight, and how the application and shortlisting process works.
Deadline
The webinar takes place on 22 October 2026 at 10:00. Applications received before 31 October join the current review wave.
Source: GOV.UK, AI Airlock Phase 3 application
