November edition · 17 to 23 November 2026

Decisions Made, Details Still to Come

Agentic, Robotics, Ambient & Clinical AI. Every week, uPull.ai tracks four categories of AI moving through the NHS: a headline, a use case, a governance note and a workflow to try. This was a quieter week for national announcements. The sources we read record decisions with detail still to follow: a security unit being set up, a robot bought for children’s surgery, an ambient scribe stopped for one group of services and a NICE consultation that has closed. We also return to four earlier stories and report what their sources say today.

This week

Last week’s edition covered what rollout numbers can tell us. This week brings ministerial answers on the National Agentic Security Unit, a children’s hospital buying its own surgical robot, a trust board paper that stops one ambient scribe, and the close of NICE’s second consultation on prostate MRI AI.

1 Agentic AI

Headline AI development

Two written parliamentary answers add detail to the Budget’s £40 million for agentic AI security, which we covered on 3 November. A Lords answer dated 12 November says the money funds “a new National Agentic Security Unit to develop new capabilities to detect, investigate and respond to agentic AI incidents”. It says the unit is being established by the National Cyber Security Centre and the answering department, and “will be housed within the NCSC”. A Commons answer dated 9 November says further information will be shared in due course. Neither answer mentions health or the NHS. Primary source quoted

Sources: Written answer HL4822, House of Lords · Written answer 39482, House of Commons
Healthcare or NHS use case

On 5 November the University of York reported that an AI assistant called Dora Care, which holds telephone consultations with patients, “is being trialled in the NHS”. The two-year project uses generative AI for calls with people who have osteoporosis. It is led by Ufonia with the universities of York and Newcastle and funded by Innovate UK. The university says the trial follows medical and ethical approval, participation is voluntary and the calls are used in addition to regular NHS care. The page does not name the NHS sites or give patient numbers. Primary source quoted

Source: University of York, 5 November 2026
Governance or ethics note

Two statements this week bear on accountability. In the York announcement, Professor Ibrahim Habli says, “Any development and deployment of AI within a healthcare setting must be backed by robust safety evidence and assurance.” In the Commons answer, the minister says the new unit’s work “will include sharing lessons learned as appropriate” and adds that the government “will not provide a running commentary on operational details”. An NHS team running an agent should agree locally how it will record and share its own incident learning. Primary source quoted

Sources: University of York · Written answer 39482
Try this in a workflow

Take one automated patient contact, such as a reminder or follow-up call. Write down who reviews the output, what triggers a hand-over to a clinician and where an incident would be recorded. Test the hand-over once with a fictional patient.

2 Robotics AI

Headline AI development

NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde reports that the Royal Hospital for Children in Glasgow “has become the first children’s hospital in the world to own a Versius Plus surgical robot dedicated exclusively to the care of children”. The purchase is funded by a pledge of more than £1.5 million from the Coldstones Charitable Trust to Hi Scotland, the innovation arm of Glasgow Children’s Hospital Charity. The page carries two dates, 27 October and 5 November 2026. The “first in the world” statement is the health board’s own. Primary source quoted

Source: NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde announcement
Healthcare or NHS use case

The health board says the purchase follows an evaluation phase in which surgeons performed more than 30 procedures with the robot. It lists complex cancer, urology, abdominal, thoracic and gastrointestinal surgery as areas of use. It says the West of Scotland Innovation Hub supported the programme through governance, evaluation and partnership working. The page gives no outcome data for the evaluation procedures. Primary source quoted

Source: NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde announcement
Governance or ethics note

On 10 November the MHRA published its weekly list of field safety notices for 2 to 6 November. The list includes an Intuitive Surgical notice dated 5 November for “SP Monopolar Curved Scissors Tip Cover”, model 430035-13. The listing does not describe the issue or the action required, and we have not read the manufacturer’s notice itself. A theatre team using robotic instruments should know who receives field safety notices in the trust and how they reach the people who handle the affected item. Primary source quoted

Source: MHRA field safety notices, 2 to 6 November 2026
Try this in a workflow

Ask the medical devices safety officer to trace one recent field safety notice from receipt to the theatre store. Record how long each step took and who confirmed the action was complete.

3 Ambient AI

Headline AI development

Lancashire and South Cumbria NHS Foundation Trust’s public board papers for 5 November include a digital update, dated 26 October, which says: “Following Trust-wide evaluation, Accurx Scribe will not be progressed for mental health services due to product stability, integration and supplier assurance concerns, with interim options being considered for current users.” The same paper says a competitive procurement is being prepared, with an updated business case due in November 2026 and procurement targeted for completion by March 2027. The paper does not describe the concerns in further detail. Primary source quoted

Source: LSCFT Board of Directors papers, 5 November 2026 (large PDF)
Healthcare or NHS use case

The same board pack reports results from the trust’s pilot of Heidi, a different ambient scribe: “87% user satisfaction, 100% of respondents willing to recommend the solution, 78% reporting an improvement in wellbeing at work and an average 45% reduction in time spent on clinical administration outside working hours.” The paper gives no sample size or pilot dates. Separately, a Contracts Finder notice published on 2 November records Barnsley Hospital NHS Foundation Trust awarding an ambient voice technology contract for acute licences to Lyrebird Health Ltd, running to 2 February 2028. The notice shows two contract values, £12,800 and £38,400, and does not explain the difference. Primary source quoted

Sources: LSCFT board papers · Contracts Finder award notice
Governance or ethics note

Assurance requirements are deciding procurement outcomes. Alongside the Lancashire decision, Digital Health and Care Wales published a notice on 20 October ending its ambient voice technology procurement. It gives the reason as “the limited number of suppliers successfully meeting the Conditions of Participation requirements”. The notice does not name the suppliers or the requirements they did not meet. A trust choosing a scribe should set its stability, integration and supplier assurance criteria before the pilot starts and score every product against them. Primary source quoted

Sources: LSCFT board papers · Digital Health and Care Wales termination notice
Try this in a workflow

List the criteria that would stop your ambient scribe pilot. Include uptime, integration with the patient record and the supplier’s assurance documents. Agree who makes the stop decision and what current users would do the next day.

4 Clinical AI

Headline AI development

An update to our 3 November story. NICE’s project page shows that consultation on the second draft of its guidance on AI tools for prostate MRI ran from 23 October to 12 November 2026 and has now closed. The status is “In progress” and the expected publication date is “TBC”. The draft recommends five technologies for NHS use during a two-year evidence-generation period. NICE says comments received will be considered by the independent committee before final guidance is published. Primary source quoted

Sources: NICE project page GID-HTG10879 · NICE news article, 23 October 2026
Healthcare or NHS use case

On 10 November Barts Health described Project VOCAL, which uses AI, eye-tracking and touch-based interaction to help intensive care patients communicate when speaking is difficult. It was developed by Anna Lazcano, an ITU nurse at St Bartholomew’s Hospital, during a Level 4 Business Analyst Apprenticeship. The trust says the project is in the development stage. The next step is to submit the initial prototype to clinicians, speech and language therapists and AI specialists “to make sure it is appropriate, safe and adhering to our governance policies”. The page does not state the tool’s regulatory status. Primary source quoted

Source: Barts Health, 10 November 2026
Governance or ethics note

South East Coast Ambulance Service published staff research on 22 October drawing on 171 survey responses and 14 in-depth interviews with its paramedics. More than 96 per cent agreed that final clinical responsibility should remain with paramedics, and 83 per cent said they would be willing to use AI clinically with appropriate safeguards. Eighty per cent said clear governance, audit processes and accountability would increase their trust in AI, and 83 per cent expressed concern about becoming overly reliant on it. These are the trust’s own summary figures. The full study is not linked from the page. Primary source quoted

Source: South East Coast Ambulance Service, 22 October 2026
Try this in a workflow

Before the next clinical AI pilot, put three of the paramedics’ questions to your own staff. Who holds final responsibility? What audit will exist? How will the team notice over-reliance? Record the answers in the pilot plan.
